The group project assignment is divided into 5 submissions that, over time, build a cohesive
prototype of an online teaching service (think Udemy or Zenva). Your group is welcome to
decide which “subject” your team plans to support.
The initial 3 submissions will be formatted as individual components that will make up your
final website. Submission 4 is a compulsory UX evaluation. The final assignment will require you
to complete the website, including all previously completed and new components.
For each submission, the group will receive a list of functions to complete and a problem
statement (a quote from the owner) to be resolved based on a specific UX design principle
covered in class.
The group is required to conduct three (per deliverable) short user experience research
sessions on an existing website (UEQ shortened questionnaire), note any criteria that scored
relatively low/high, and use this to formulate a set of design guidelines. This will also help you
establish common feature trends or existing practices used within the industry.

The website's general operations should allow users to log in, browse courses, add courses to a
cart, check out, and track their course progression.
Note: You do not need to create a teaching system for the content itself! You are creating a shell
of what the website's general operations and experience should look like. You are welcome to
add “placeholders” to replace certain functionalities (e.g., the learning system, the course
content, etc.).
In addition to these general operations, your group needs to determine what other features
might be needed and implement them accordingly. Remember, this is all about user experience.

Markers need to see and be able to interact with all elements of the website. Each
button/link/operation needs to “operate” according to your final vision. This includes things
like loading/progress bars, feedback on elements, error handling, real images, etc. You can think
of it as developing the full, working front end of your website.
As explained in the study guide, completeness is a major criterion in the evaluation of all
assignments. This means each component should be complete in both functionality and
presentation.
● Functionality – would include features and capabilities that allow users to interact with
and perform tasks on the site, such as navigating pages, submitting forms, and accessing
dynamic content. (Even if they don’t communicate with a backend, they need to appear
that they do)
● Presentation – would refer to the visual and structural aspects of the site, including
layout, design, and user interface elements, which ensure that all necessary information
is displayed and accessible to users. (Including all animations and notifications)
