UX Report: Strumly
Summary
Our guitar-learning site is easy to use, but saving courses and searching for them need fixing. The sign-in page needs some work such as password confirmation, and when it comes to the UI, we need to separate sections with colour instead of lines. All four testers finished all five tasks without help (20 out of 20). The UEQ-S scored usability positive (+1.19) and excitement neutral (-0.13), so people find the site clear but not especially exciting.
Two problems affected every tester:
* Saved courses are hard to find. The save icon is only inside the course page, and the saved list is under the profile hover.
* There is no genre filter. Testers had to type the genre into search, and all four paused on this task.
Five smaller problems affected one to three testers each: search only matches names, nothing helps users choose a course, instructors cannot be filtered or given their own page, features are hard to see on the dark background, and sign-up has no confirm-password field. The pain point table below gives the evidence and a question to solve for each.
What we did
Four people tested the site one at a time. Each answered a few questions about how they learn and choose courses, did five tasks, filled in the UEQ-S, and answered debrief questions. The tasks said what to do but not how to do it:
1. Start a course (find a course and start it)
2. Resume a course
3. Save and find a course (save a course for later and find it again)
4. Find an instructor
5. Search for a blues course

	Saving (Task 3) and searching (Task 5) caused 20 of the 32 wrong clicks and every pause. Everyone finished every task, so completion alone does not show these problems. What happened in each task:
* Task 1: quick, with no pauses. P1 and P3 picked the first course shown.
* Task 2: the resume button was where testers expected it, and all four used the progress bar.
* Task 3: the slowest task. Every tester had 3 wrong clicks, 3 of 4 paused, and confidence was lowest.
* Task 4: quick, but testers looked for an instructor filter or an instructor page.
* Task 5: all four paused, and with no filter, testers had to type the genre into the search bar.

	What worked well
* Progress bar: all four testers named it as how they knew their progress.
* Resume button: it was where testers expected it.
* Completion animation: the best moment for P1, P3 and P4. The Peak-End Rule says people remember the best moment, so it is worth protecting.
* Streaks and XP: P1 said they would keep them motivated.
* Look and feel: P1 and P3 said it was easy to use, and P3 and P4 said it looked professional.
Priorities for the redesign
1. Solve UX-1 and UX-2 first, because every tester hit them on a main task.
2. Then UX-3 and UX-4, which affected half or more of the testers.
3. Treat UX-5 to UX-7 as lower priority, and test sign-up properly..
